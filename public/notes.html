CHEMISTRY GRADE 11 - UNIT 6

Alcohols, Ethers, Aldehydes, Ketones, Carboxylic Acids, Esters, Fats and Oils

6.0 Introduction

Organic compounds containing oxygen are crucial in chemistry. This unit covers six major functional groups: alcohols (–OH), ethers (–O–), aldehydes (–CHO), ketones (–CO–), carboxylic acids (–COOH), and esters (–COOR).

Learning Outcomes

  • ✅ Understand structure and nomenclature of oxygen-containing compounds
  • ✅ Explain physical and chemical properties
  • ✅ Prepare these compounds in laboratory
  • ✅ Identify functional groups and their reactions
  • ✅ Apply knowledge to real-world applications

6.1 ALCOHOLS

Alcohols are organic compounds containing one or more hydroxyl functional groups (–OH) attached to a saturated carbon atom.

Classification

  • Primary: –OH on carbon with 1 alkyl group
  • Secondary: –OH on carbon with 2 alkyl groups
  • Tertiary: –OH on carbon with 3 alkyl groups

Nomenclature

IUPAC Rule: Replace "–e" of alkane with "–ol"
Example: ethane → ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH)

Physical Properties

  • ✅ Polar due to –OH group
  • ✅ Strong hydrogen bonding → high boiling points
  • ✅ Soluble in water (especially lower alcohols)
  • ✅ Solubility decreases with chain length

Preparation

1. Hydration of Alkenes (Markovnikov's Rule): CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O → CH₃CH₂OH (H₂SO₄ catalyst, heat)
2. Reduction of Aldehydes/Ketones: RCHO + 2H → RCH₂OH (Primary alcohol)
3. Hydrolysis of Alkyl Halides: R–X + NaOH → R–OH + NaX

Chemical Reactions

A. Oxidation:
• Primary alcohols → aldehydes → carboxylic acids
• Secondary alcohols → ketones
• Tertiary alcohols → no oxidation (stable)
B. Dehydration (C–O bond broken):
R–OH → C=C + H₂O (conc. H₂SO₄, 170°C)
C. Reaction with Active Metals:
2ROH + 2Na → 2RONa + H₂↑
D. Esterification:
ROH + R'COOH ⇌ RCOOR' + H₂O (H₂SO₄ catalyst)

Uses

  • Ethanol: Fuel, solvent, antiseptic
  • Methanol: Industrial solvent
  • Glycerol: Moisturizer, food additive

6.2 ETHERS

Ethers are compounds where oxygen is bonded to two alkyl or aryl groups (R–O–R').

Nomenclature

Common Names: alkyl + alkyl + ether
Example: CH₃–O–C₂H₅ = ethyl methyl ether

IUPAC Names: alkoxyalkane
Example: CH₃–O–C₂H₅ = methoxyethane

Physical Properties

  • ✅ Weakly polar (no hydrogen bonding between molecules)
  • ✅ Lower boiling points than alcohols
  • ✅ Slightly soluble in water
  • ✅ Good organic solvents

Preparation

1. Dehydration of Alcohols (140°C): 2ROH → R–O–R + H₂O (conc. H₂SO₄)
2. Williamson Ether Synthesis (SN2): R–X + R'O⁻ → R–O–R' + X⁻

Reactions

A. Cleavage by Strong Acids:
R–O–R' + 2HBr → 2RBr + H₂O (heat)
B. Combustion:
Ether + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

Uses

  • Diethyl ether: Anesthetic, solvent
  • Solvents in organic synthesis
  • Industrial applications

6.3 ALDEHYDES & KETONES

Aldehydes

Aldehydes contain a carbonyl group (C=O) bonded to at least one hydrogen atom at the end of a carbon chain.
General Formula: R–CHO
Nomenclature: Replace "–e" with "–al"
Example: methanal (HCHO), ethanal (CH₃CHO)

Ketones

Ketones have the carbonyl group (C=O) between two carbon atoms.
General Formula: R–CO–R'
Nomenclature: Replace "–e" with "–one"
Example: propanone (CH₃COCH₃), butanone (CH₃CH₂COCH₃)

Physical Properties

Property Aldehyde/Ketone Reason
Boiling Point Higher than alkanes, lower than alcohols Dipole–dipole interactions (no H-bonding)
Solubility Lower members soluble in water H-bonding with water through C=O
Odor Sharp (aldehydes), fruity (ketones) Volatile polar compounds

Reactions

OXIDATION: • Aldehydes → Carboxylic acids (KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇) • Ketones → No easy oxidation
REDUCTION: • Aldehydes → Primary alcohols • Ketones → Secondary alcohols
CONDENSATION: • With NH₂NH₂ → Hydrazone • With NaHSO₃ → Bisulfite addition

6.4 CARBOXYLIC ACIDS

Carboxylic acids contain the carboxyl group (–COOH), which consists of a carbonyl group (C=O) and a hydroxyl group (–OH) on the same carbon.
General Formula: R–COOH
IUPAC Nomenclature: Replace "–e" with "–oic acid"
Example: methanoic acid (HCOOH), ethanoic acid (CH₃COOH)

Classification

  • Monocarboxylic acids (1 –COOH)
  • Dicarboxylic acids (2 –COOH)
  • Tricarboxylic acids (3 –COOH)

Physical Properties

  • ✅ Very high boiling points (hydrogen bonding + dimer formation)
  • ✅ Completely miscible (C₁–C₄ with water)
  • ✅ Form hydrogen-bonded dimers
  • ✅ Pungent odor (especially lower homologs)

Acidic Nature

RCOOH ⇌ RCOO⁻ + H⁺
Weak acids (partially ionize in water)
Strength increases with electron-withdrawing groups

Reactions

1. SALT FORMATION: 2RCOOH + 2Na → 2RCOONa + H₂↑ RCOOH + NaOH → RCOONa + H₂O
2. ESTERIFICATION: RCOOH + R'OH ⇌ RCOOR' + H₂O (H₂SO₄)
3. DECARBOXYLATION: RCOOH → RH + CO₂ (strong heat, lime)
4. HALOGENATION: RCH₂COOH + X₂ → RCHXCOOH + HX (PCl₃)

Preparation

Oxidation Method (Most Important):
• Primary alcohols + strong oxidant → carboxylic acids
• Aldehyde + KMnO₄ → carboxylic acids
• Alkylbenzenes + KMnO₄ (heat) → benzoic acids

Fatty Acids

Saturated Fatty Acids: No C=C bonds (solid at room temp)
Example: Stearic acid (18 carbons, 69°C melting point)

Unsaturated Fatty Acids: C=C bonds present (liquid at room temp)
Example: Oleic acid (1 double bond, 13°C melting point)

Reason: Double bonds create kinks, reducing packing efficiency

6.5 ESTERS

Esters are compounds formed when the hydrogen of a carboxylic acid is replaced by an alkyl group. They have the general formula R–COO–R'.

Nomenclature

Common Name: alkyl (from alcohol) + acid name
Example: CH₃COOCH₃ = methyl acetate

IUPAC Name: alkyl + alkanoate
Example: CH₃COOCH₃ = methyl ethanoate

Preparation (Esterification)

RCOOH + R'OH ⇌ RCOOR' + H₂O
(conc. H₂SO₄, heat, Fischer esterification)

Physical Properties

  • ✅ Pleasant fruity smell
  • ✅ Lower boiling points than acids/alcohols
  • ✅ Slightly soluble in water
  • ✅ Soluble in organic solvents

Reactions

1. ACID-CATALYZED HYDROLYSIS: RCOOR' + H₂O ⇌ RCOOH + R'OH (H⁺)
2. ALKALINE HYDROLYSIS (SAPONIFICATION): RCOOR' + NaOH → RCOONa + R'OH (Produces soap from fat esters)
3. REDUCTION: RCOOR' + LiAlH₄ → RCH₂OH + R'OH

Uses

  • Artificial flavoring (fruit smell)
  • Perfumes & fragrances
  • Polyesters (synthetic fibers)
  • Solvents (paint, varnish)

6.6 FATS & OILS

Fats and oils are triglycerides (triacylglycerols)—esters of glycerol with three long-chain fatty acids.

Structure

Glycerol (C₃H₈O₃) + 3 Fatty Acids → Triglyceride

Glycerol has 3 –OH groups that form ester bonds with 3 different fatty acids.

Fats vs Oils

Property Fats Oils
Saturation Saturated (no C=C) Unsaturated (C=C present)
State Solid at room temp Liquid at room temp
Melting Point Higher (69°C+) Lower (−5 to 13°C)
Examples Butter, lard, animal fats Olive, corn, sunflower oils

Hydrogenation (Hardening Oils)

Unsaturated oil + H₂ → Saturated fat
(Ni catalyst, heat, high pressure)

Produces: Margarine, solid cooking fats
Reason: Converts liquid oils to solid fats

Rancidity

Definition: Spoilage of fats/oils with unpleasant smell & taste

Causes:
• Hydrolysis of ester bonds (moisture)
• Oxidation of C=C bonds (air/oxygen)
• Heat degradation

Prevention:
• Store in airtight containers
• Keep away from heat & light
• Use antioxidants (e.g., BHA, BHT)
• Maintain proper hygiene

Saponification

Fat/Oil + NaOH → Glycerol + Soap (sodium carboxylate)
RCOOR' + NaOH → RCOONa + R'OH

Used in: Soap making, detergent production

Uses

  • Energy source (9 kcal/gram)
  • Soap & detergent production
  • Cooking oils
  • Margarine & butter alternatives
  • Cosmetics & skincare
  • Industrial lubricants

UNIT 6 SUMMARY

✅ Alcohols: –OH group, soluble in water, can be oxidized
✅ Ethers: –O– linkage, weakly polar, inert solvents
✅ Aldehydes: Terminal C=O, easily oxidized to acids
✅ Ketones: Internal C=O, resist oxidation
✅ Carboxylic Acids: –COOH, weakly acidic, form dimers
✅ Esters: Fruity smell, formed from acid + alcohol
✅ Fats/Oils: Triglycerides, saturated (solid) vs unsaturated (liquid)

REVIEW QUESTIONS (1–15)

1. Which alcohol cannot undergo oxidation easily?
Tertiary alcohols
2. Ethers are formed by dehydration of alcohols at what temperature?
140°C (with conc. H₂SO₄)
3. Name the compound CH₃COCH₂CH₃ (IUPAC)?
Butanone
4. Which gives positive Tollens' test?
Aldehydes (not ketones)
5. Carboxylic acids form dimers due to?
Hydrogen bonding
6. Saponification is used to make?
Soap (from fats/oils)
7. Unsaturated oils are converted to fats by?
Hydrogenation (adding H₂)
8. Primary alcohol → ? → ? (on oxidation)
Aldehyde → Carboxylic acid
9. Why is olive oil liquid but butter solid?
Olive oil is unsaturated (C=C bonds cause kinks)
10. Esterification requires catalyst?
Concentrated H₂SO₄
11. Which compound has the highest boiling point (same MW)?
Carboxylic acid (hydrogen bonding + dimer)
12. Ethers do NOT react with?
KMnO₄, strong bases (very inert)
13. HCOOH is called (common name)?
Formic acid
14. C₆H₅COOH is?
Benzoic acid (aromatic carboxylic acid)
15. Rancidity is prevented by?
Airtight storage, keeping away from heat/light, using antioxidants
